What it does

Five things. That’s the whole app.

Browse

266 recipes you can actually find

Filter by book, section, effort, or whether you’d have to buy something. Scale any recipe from a quarter up to eight times, and you get fractions back, not decimals.

Plan

Plan a week and it stays planned

There are no dates. Monday is just Monday, nothing clears itself, and you can keep as many weeks as you want.

Shop

The list adds itself up

A diced apple and a sliced apple are both just apples. Three recipes that want cottage cheese come out as one line: 2⅛ cups.

Pantry

What you actually have

It starts as the storehouse order sheet. Take things off as you stop keeping them and every recipe updates what it says you’d need to buy. That’s what keeps this useful after you stop going.

Print

A real book, not a printout

Half-letter size, with a contents page and proper typesetting. No recipe ever gets cut in half by a page break.

All of it’s free, and it stays free. If it earns its keep you can pay what you think it’s worth, or have me print one and post it.

On your phones

No app store, no account. This is the part people like best.

Install it straight from the page. In Safari, Share → Add to Home Screen. In Chrome, ⋮ → Install app. You get an icon, and the recipes are saved on the phone, so it still opens in a storehouse basement with no signal.

Then share a code. Everything saves on the phone you’re using. Tap the badge, take the code it gives you, and type it on the other phone. That’s it.

Now your favorites, your weeks and your ticked-off groceries are the same on both phones. You can both edit at once. If one of you is offline it catches up when you’re back.
