What it does

Five things, and it tries to do them properly rather than do twelve badly.

Browse

263 recipes, sorted how you think

Filter by book, section, effort, or whether a recipe needs anything the storehouse does not carry. Every one scales from ¼× to 8×, and fractions stay fractions.

Plan

A week that stays where you put it

No dates. Monday is a slot, not a date, and nothing resets on its own. Keep as many weeks as you like and rotate through them.

Shop

A list that adds up for you

A diced apple and a sliced apple are apples. Three recipes wanting cottage cheese ask for 2⅛ cups, not three separate lines.

Pantry

What you actually keep in

Starts as the storehouse order. Take things off as you stop keeping them and every recipe changes its mind about what you would have to go out for — so the book outlives the storehouse.

Print

An actual book, not a printout

Half-letter, typeset, with front matter and its own contents. Pages are measured in the browser and packed to fit, so a recipe never splits across a leaf.

One code, two phones

The part everyone likes best.

Out of the box, everything saves in the browser you opened it in — your phone and your spouse’s each keep their own. Tap the badge, take the code it offers, and type that same code on the other phone. That is the whole of it.

Favorites, every week you have planned, which one is showing, and the shopping-list check-offs are now the same on both. Both editing at once is fine — adding a recipe to Tuesday touches only Tuesday, and ticking off milk touches only milk. Changes made offline queue up and go out when you reconnect.

The code is the key — there is no password, so keep it between the two of you. What is in there is a grocery list: no names, no addresses, nothing to pay with.

Put it on your phone

There is no app store involved. It installs from the page itself.

  1. Open the app in Safari on iPhone, or Chrome on Android.
  2. Tap Share → Add to Home Screen on iPhone, or ⋮ → Install app on Android.
  3. That is it. It gets an icon and opens without browser chrome, and the recipes, the styling and both typefaces are kept on the phone — so it opens in a storehouse basement with no signal rather than showing a browser error.
