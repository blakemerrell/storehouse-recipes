What it does
1

Find a recipe, plan a week, shop from it

Filter by effort or by what you’d have to buy. Scale any recipe up to eight times and get fractions back, not decimals. Plan a week — it just stays planned, no dates to expire. The list builds itself and adds duplicates together, so three recipes wanting cottage cheese come out as one line: 2⅛ cups.

2

Tell it what’s actually in your pantry

It starts as the storehouse order sheet. Take things off as you stop keeping them, and every recipe updates what it says you’d need to buy — the part that keeps this useful after you stop going.

3

Print it as a real book

Half-letter, with a contents page and proper typesetting. No recipe is ever cut in half by a page break, and the files are rendered ahead of time, so there’s no print dialog to argue with about paper size.

Where this came from

My wife and I are using the bishops’ storehouse right now.

It’s crazy. I don’t feel like I should be in this position. My salary has been enough for twenty years, and all of a sudden it isn’t. My mortgage is smaller than most. I don’t have a car payment. I have two young kids and a wonderful wife who wants to be with them as they grow up, not outsource that responsibility to a day care. We do what we can to be helpful to our church and our neighborhood. I show up every day to a good job that pays decent. We should be able to make it. But here we are.

Part of how we got here: it was 2020, and we wanted to be closer to family and closer to home, so we packed up in the middle of the pandemic and moved back to Utah. Grandparents nearby, a promising opportunity with a new job — everything looked like it was going right.

And for a while it was. We were renting a single-wide from extended family and we were fine. What we could not do was have another child. Infertility kept us at one, we spent tens of thousands of dollars on doctors, and we lost a pregnancy, which about destroyed us. In the end they could not help us. So we decided to be content with what we had, and to build our house.

Things were lining up. And then, once we had the permits in hand, our second decided to be born. So we were building a house and having a baby at the same time — and getting my startup AS9100 certified through all of it, by myself.

The number for the build came back at over half a million dollars. That was never going to be a number we had.

It frustrated me enough that I decided I would do it myself. I’m no homebuilder, but I spent years in the roof truss industry, so construction and building materials and nail guns don’t scare me. And my family showed up in a way I still can’t get over. One brother-in-law brought his excavation equipment. Another works at his family’s lumber and hardware store. My brother came weekend after weekend, and sometimes every day for weeks, setting roof and laying sheathing. My dad came nearly every day he could and did the rough electrical for the whole house. The whole house. It blows me away.

So when I went to my bishop, it was after doing everything I knew how to do.

And even with this help, it’s hard to make ends meet, and the debt still seems to overwhelm us. I’m working hard to make my startup work, and I have a few other ideas for bringing in extra income.

After a few months of using the storehouse, I was looking at all this food and wondering how I could make amazing food at home that my kids would love, with the ingredients I already had here. After some work, I came up with this.

It’s helpful to us, and I hope it’s helpful to you. We love the Lord, and we are glad to put this in the hands of anybody it might help. It is free, and it stays free.
