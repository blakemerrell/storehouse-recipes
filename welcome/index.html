What it does
1

Cook from a short list

The storehouse carries about a hundred things. Everything here is built from them, so the question stops being what do I feel like and becomes what can I actually make. Pick your meals for the week and they stay picked; the shopping list builds itself, and three recipes calling for cottage cheese come out as one line: 2⅛ cups. Scale any recipe up to eight times.

2

Know what to order, and what to go out for

Every recipe says at the foot of it whether the storehouse covers the whole thing, or names exactly what it does not carry — before you start, not halfway through. Filter the whole collection down to what needs nothing bought elsewhere. And the week’s shopping list comes out in two parts: what to order, and the short separate list for the one run to the grocery store.

3

Every recipe has a score

Out of 100, built from protein, calories, fat, sodium, fiber and how much of it is refined carbohydrate. A green leaf means cook it often. Grey means know what you’re getting. I put the cutoffs near the middle of the collection so the score actually sorts it. Tap the leaf and it shows its working. Write your own recipe and it gets scored the same way.

4

The list changes; so can this

The order is not the same everywhere and it does not stay the same. When your storehouse stops carrying something, take it off and every recipe in both volumes updates what it says you’d need to buy. The same switch is what keeps this useful if you stop going altogether.

5

Or take it off the screen entirely

Half-letter, with a contents page and real typesetting. Recipes don’t get split across a page break. The PDFs are already made, so you send one to a printer and that’s it — 277 recipes over 176 pages. Or have a spiral-bound copy posted to you; the address is taken at checkout.

Other ways to print it

Or take it as two booklets you fold and staple yourself. Run and Not Be Weary is 111 recipes over 64 pages (PDF · imposed for folding). Around the Table is 166 over 120 (PDF · imposed for folding). The imposed files are deliberately out of page order, so don’t hand one to a copy shop for spiral binding.

Handing these out? There’s a one-sheet handout — eight storehouse-only recipes over both sides, with the code on each, made to be photocopied and sent home with an order (PDF).

Where this came from

My wife and I have needed the bishops’ storehouse a couple of times now.

Once in the 1900s, when I was on the wrong side of a RIF. And again now, with what used to be a good salary and no way to make ends meet on one income. We should be able to make it. But here we are.

Part of how we got here: we moved back to Utah in 2020 to be near family, and it looked right. Then it wasn’t. Infertility kept us at one child, we spent tens of thousands on doctors, and we lost a pregnancy, which about destroyed us. So we decided to be content with what we had and build our house — and once we had the permits, our second decided to be born. A house and a baby at the same time, and getting my startup AS9100 certified through all of it. Then the build came back at over half a million, so I built it myself. Years in the roof truss industry; nail guns don’t scare me.

And my family showed up. One brother-in-law brought his excavation equipment, another works at his family’s lumber and hardware store. My brother came weekend after weekend, setting roof and laying sheathing. My dad came nearly every day he could and did the rough electrical for the whole house. The whole house. It blows me away.

So when I went to my bishop, it was after doing everything I knew how to do.

A few months into the storehouse order, I was looking at all that food and wondering what I could make from it that my kids would love. This is what came of it.

It’s helpful to us, and I hope it’s helpful to you. We love the Lord, and we are glad to put this in the hands of anybody it might help.
